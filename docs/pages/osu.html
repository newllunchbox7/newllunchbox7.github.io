OSU!

Gear

Out of most of the rhythm games that will be covered here OSU! requires the least amount of gear.

To play OSU! you only need a couple of things:

1) A drawing tablet (or mouse)

2) A MECHANICAL or HALL EFFECT keyboard

3) A computer

aside from the computer these should be relatively cheap to get.

Tablets

When buying a tablet, in general you want one from Wacom as they are generaly higher quality than other brands (gaomon, huion, xp-pen, etc) though it doesn't matter a whole bunch.
but if you want suggestions or info on what tablets to buy you can use kuuube's tablet buying guide. Your tablet will also need software to properly work. You can install the proprietary software that comes with the tablet but my personal reccomendation is to use opentabletdriver as it works on all platforms and works with almost every tablet.

Keyboards

keyboards are a little bit easier to buy compared to a tablet. Almost any keyboard works. However if you end up with a membrane keyboard do note that it feels MUCH worse to play on. In general a mechanical keyboard is your best option. If you have a bit more money to spare though you can also get a hall effect keyboard which is the best performance wise.
if you want a bit more info before buying you can check the Public VSRG Keyboard Sheet.
although it's ment for VSRGs like OSU!Mania(<3) or Etterna, some of the information such as polling rate can be relevant for OSU!

Computer

Now OSU! is pretty lightweight compared to most modern games. To run OSU! (more specifically OSU!Lazer) you need a 64 bit computer with windows, mac, or linux(with appimage support) 8gb of ram (though you could play with less) and OpenGL2.1+ or DirectX 11. For storage it can take up anywhere from 1gb - 10gb (my install is about 10gb) for most users (though if you do want to horde beatmaps like a madman it could take up 80gb+)

Basic setup and things you should do before playing

With all the hardware that you need to play OSU! out of the way we'll set up the game and some settings.

Installation

installing the game and going through the wizard

To install OSU! all you need to do is to navigate to osu.ppy.sh/home/download and hit big purple download button.

Once you have downloaded the installer, (or with linux the appimage,) run the installer and once the game finishes installing, launch the game.
Once you are in the game it will prompt you with the first-run setup. You should go through this and be sure to read EVERYTHING it tells you as it is important.

During the setup, make sure to install the tutorial. (you will NEED to play this in order to understand the game)

When the game prompts you with this login screen, hit register and go through the account creation.
THIS IS REQUIRED.
Without an account you will be restricted to LOCAL PLAY ONLY and will be UNABLE TO DOWNLOAD BEATMAPS except for the ones that install during the setup wizard.

Now that your settings are changed, you now get to download and try out some skins.
The easiest way to get skins is to go to skins.osuck.net Here you can find almost every OSU! skin. Look around and try to find one that looks good to you.

Once you find a skin you like, open the skin's page then head to the download tab. From there, it should give you multiple versions of the skin. (or sometimes only one) From there you will need to click on the download button that says "osk" (which is OSU!'s skin file) After you click it it will give you options on where to download the skin from. You can pick any of them. Once the .osk is done downloading, open your file manager and open it. (double click) It should automatically import into OSU!

You can change your skin by opening the settings then navigating to the 'skins' tab on the left then clicking the skin dropdown.
Do note that not all skins support all gamemodes and you will have to check which modes it supports on osuck.
Some skins might have a tag before the name such as <NM1>,<NM2>, or <DT> typically this refers to the type of skin it is. For example NM1, 2, or 3 stand for No Mod 1, 2, or 3 which refers to playing OSU! with no mods enabled such as: Double Time(DT), Hardrock(HR), or Hidden(HD) And you probably can figure out that <DT> refers to a skin that you would use when playing Double Time. There can also be other tags, but those usually are the skinner's name or a player's name.

Gameplay and playstyle

Now that you have the setup and the tutorial finished it's time to get into the actual game.

Basic gameplay advice

Basic gameplay advice that helps you play better in the long run.

!!!THIS PARAGRAPH IS FOR MOUSE PLAY ONLY!!

There are TONS of people who say that you CANNOT get good if you play on a mouse or that tablet is far better than mouse. The first statement is completely wrong. (The #3 player as of 9/30/26, ivaxa plays on a mouse.) The second statement is more personal prefrence than actual fact. You can and will get good even if you play on a mouse. The only thing that matters is how much you play.

Some things you should note when you first start playing with a mouse. You might start to play with mouse buttons, though instead what you should use are the Z,X keys on your keyboard. In the future this will help you click faster, have better stamina, and be more consistant with your clicks. If Z and X feel uncomfortable to press you can rebind the keys in the settings menu. (mine are rebound to A,S) Also when playing you could experience what's called 'mouse drift' which is when you cursor "drifts" away from where you expect it to be. This happens because a mouse works by moving your mouse relative to your movement whereas a tablet uses an absolute area where each spot on the tablet is mapped to a spot on the monitor.

!!!THIS PARAGRAPH IS FOR TABLET PLAY ONLY!!!

When you first start playing on a tablet, the area (which is the section of the tablet where the pen actually tracks) will be set to whats called 'full area.' Full area means that the screen will be mapped to the ENTIRE trackable area of the tablet which may be fine for smaller tablets but for medium to large size tablets (such as my Wacom PTH-650) full area is too large to play on. You can fix this by going into opentabletdriver and adjusting your width, height, X offset, and y offset. if you don't want to experiment on your own you can copy other people's settings but keep in mind that the X and Y offset will be relative to YOUR tablet area unlike width and height which are in mm.
(my area is 55x55mm square)
